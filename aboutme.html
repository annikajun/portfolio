Hello! I'm Annika, an Interaction Designer based in Ireland.

My core interests are in the intersection of digital fabrication and creative coding. My work involves interactive programming, prototyping, and crafting engaging screen-based content. I approach every project with curiosity, creativity, and a hands-on drive, aiming to design thoughtful, tech-driven solutions that make a real impact.

Design Statement

I am an Interaction Designer with a particular interest in the space where physical making meets digital thinking. My practice sits at the intersection of creative technology, physical computing, and human-centred design — and what draws me most is the moment when an idea stops being a sketch and becomes something you can hold, press, wear, or step inside.

Growing up between cultures — German and Irish — gave me an early appreciation for systems and how things connect. That curiosity carried into my studies in Interaction Design, where I found myself equally at home soldering circuits and building wireframes, scripting in Max/MSP and planning user flows. I do not see these as separate disciplines; for me, they are just different tools in service of the same question: how can this experience feel more alive, more useful, or more human?

The projects I am most proud of are the ones that pushed me to think across categories. DeskStop started as a 3D-printed housing project and ended as a live data prototype exploring public transit anxiety. ViperRing began as an audio brief and became a meditation on AI identity and sound. LitNest asked me to design not just screens, but the logic of what helps readers build habits. Each of those projects taught me that the most interesting design problems resist a single toolkit.

What drives me is the challenge of taking something complex — a system, a feeling, a behaviour — and making it feel simple and considered for the person on the other end. I care deeply about accessibility, honesty in design, and not adding noise to an already noisy world. I am motivated by constraints, by prototyping quickly and learning from failure, and by the kind of feedback that changes the direction of a project completely.

My values as a designer come down to three things: make it real early, keep the human in the loop, and never mistake polish for quality. I would rather show a rough working model in week two than a beautiful mockup in week eight. I believe good design is collaborative, iterative, and always slightly unfinished — there is always another question worth asking.

Looking ahead, I want to work in environments where emerging technologies — spatial computing, AI-assisted tools, physical interfaces — are being used thoughtfully and critically. I am excited by the possibilities of immersive and embodied experience design, and I want to be part of teams that are asking not just "can we build this?" but "should we, and how should it feel?" My goal is to keep making things that surprise me, and hopefully, the people who use them too.
